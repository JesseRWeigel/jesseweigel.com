At 00:09 this morning the server log printed the line this entire campaign has been marching toward:

```
Forge has made the advancement [We Need to Go Deeper]
```

Enter the Nether. Nineteen of one hundred twenty-two, and the end of the longest arc this project has run.

## The last night

The finish came in a rush after two weeks of grind. Yesterday evening Atlas was standing at the village with all ten obsidian blocks in his pack — and his own brain kept sending him a hundred blocks the wrong way, toward a half-built frame some confused teammate had cast beside a deep lava pool. The build order had a hole in it: the "go help the biggest frame" rule ran before the "you are literally carrying a whole portal" rule.

Once that was flipped, the dominoes fell in a single evening, each one exposing the next:

- Atlas walked home and placed the first frame blocks the project has ever placed by hand — five, then eight.
- The top bar stumped him: those two blocks hang over the doorway with nothing beneath them. The fix borrowed the old casting rig's trick — pillar dirt up the doorway, set the obsidian on top, and let the doorway-clearing step haul the dirt back out.
- The clearing step then failed in silence, twice, because the digger was standing out of reach the whole time. Taught to walk up first, it cleared the doorway in one pass, and the flint and steel finally had something to spark against.
- The portal lit. And then three bots in a row stood beside the glowing doorway, politely reporting that someone ought to walk in. The pathfinder refuses to path into a portal block — the dimension change would rip the world out from under it — so nobody ever took the last step.

The last step turned out to need the oldest tool in the box: let go of the pathfinder, face the doorway, hold forward, and stand still for four seconds.

## The crossing

Forge — who mined the diamonds, crafted the second pickaxe, and quarried a third of the obsidian — walked into the purple and vanished. The advancement fired the moment he arrived on the far side, along with the Nether chapter's opening point: two advancements for one brave step. He then discovered that the trip home suffers the same doorway physics, and stood at the return portal like a commuter watching a train leave. That fix shipped within the hour; the pioneer walks home the same way he left.

The portal itself is everything the plan asked for: at the village, eight blocks from the team stash, registered in the swarm's shared memory, and protected — every pickaxe in the fleet is forbidden from touching those ten blocks, forever.

## What it took

The scoreboard says one line moved. Underneath it: a demolished first frame, a rebuilt supply chain, five separate walks armored against mob panics, a mint that casts obsidian at lava and carries it home, and roughly four hundred and twenty runs of a five-bot swarm figuring out one link at a time. The Nether is open now, and it stays open.

Nineteen of one hundred twenty-two, every point earned. The next chapter has fortresses in it.
