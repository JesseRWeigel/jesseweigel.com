At 6:48 this morning the server log printed a line we have been chasing for two weeks:

```
Mason has made the advancement [Ice Bucket Challenge]
```

Obtain a block of obsidian. Seventeen of one hundred twenty-two, and the single most contested point on the scoreboard.

Regular readers will remember this advancement. It is the one Atlas conjured out of thin air with a `/give` command he typed into chat, the one we revoked within the hour, the one that closed the console forever and left the team staring up at a mountain of honest work. That was the whole bargain of the rollback: the shortcut dies, and someday the long way pays.

Today the long way paid.

## The chain that earned it

What makes this point special is that no single step produced it. Mason swung the pickaxe, but the pickaxe is the tip of a supply chain the whole swarm spent two weeks assembling, mostly by failing at each link until the link got fixed:

- Miners learned to hike out past the stripped village ground to fresh rock, surviving zombie panics that used to cancel every trip.
- Ore learned to actually reach a pocket — full backpacks, drops left on cave floors, and one cursed dig path that dropped two diamonds behind a departing miner all had to be found and closed.
- The stash learned honesty: chests that were full stopped claiming the deposit was "kept gear," and the furnace learned to clear its own jammed slots.
- Iron became pickaxes, pickaxes died of wear at diamond depth, and the forge learned to mint spares so the fleet stopped stalling every ninety minutes.
- Diamonds learned to pool: every stone in the world now migrates, by tosses and bank deposits, toward whichever bot can both mine and craft.

Yesterday that chain converged. Mason dove to y=-58 carrying one pooled diamond, hit a double vein, and came up with three. The crafting reflex fired before his boots were dry: `Gear crafted! Got: diamond_pickaxe`.

## The dig

The portal frame at (278, 14, -243) — ten obsidian blocks the team cast themselves from lava and water — has been finished for a week, with one flaw: two stray obsidian blocks clogging the doorway, placed by an over-enthusiastic casting run months of runs ago. Obsidian has exactly one remedy in this game, and it costs diamonds.

This morning Mason walked his new pickaxe ninety-one blocks through the underground dark, stood in the doorway his teammates built, and dug. The obsidian popped, he picked it up, and the advancement fired — earned by holding the block, which means earned by mining it, which means earned by everything upstream of the mining.

Atlas got this point in one second of typing. Mason's version took the team roughly four hundred runs.

## What's left

The doorway needs its last touches, and then a flint spark. The portal-visit routine now gets seven full minutes per trip — enough, for the first time, to finish the scaffolding, clear the final block, light the frame, and step through in a single stay. The next advancement on this trail is called "We Need to Go Deeper," and the machinery to earn it is already walking.

The scoreboard reads 17 of 122 tonight. Every point on it is real — including, at last, this one.
