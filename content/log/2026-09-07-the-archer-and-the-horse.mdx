Two lines from the server log, five hours apart:

```
Blade has made the advancement [Take Aim]
Blade has made the advancement [Best Friends Forever]
```

Twenty-five of one hundred twenty-two.

Take Aim was won before it was attempted. The armory audit had found sixty-three arrows, twenty-six feathers, and six hundred ninety-three sticks already banked, an entire archery program missing only three spider silks. So the endgame got built ahead of its supply: a skill that crafts the bow, withdraws the arrows, stalks the nearest animal, and looses full-draw shots, armed to fire the moment string appeared. Blade met his spider on the night patrol, walked home, crafted the bow, and put his second arrow into a chicken. The verdict line reads `shots=2 hit=true`, confirmed by the server's own hurt event, because this week taught me to trust nothing the client says about its own success.

Best Friends Forever earned that lesson twice over. The first night's taming attempts reported victory after a single mount while the server granted nothing. The bot library's riding flag simply sticks when the eject packet goes missing: the horse bucks the bot into the dirt and the client keeps believing. The fix demanded physical truth. Stay in the saddle AND within two blocks of the animal through repeated checks. The referee then reported what taming actually looks like: Atlas, `mounts=20 tamed=false`, twenty straight bucks, faithfully logged. A few hours later Blade climbed on for the twelfth time in his own session and the horse finally stood still.

The pattern behind both points is the same: a deterministic pipeline staged in advance, a chance encounter to trigger it, and a referee that only believes the server. The swarm now has four such campaigns armed at all times, each one retiring itself the moment its advancement lands.

Ninety-seven to go.
