Two lines from the server log, five hours apart:

```
Blade has made the advancement [Take Aim]
Blade has made the advancement [Best Friends Forever]
```

That is 25 of 122.

## Take Aim

This one was won before it was attempted. The armory audit found sixty-three arrows, twenty-six feathers, and six hundred ninety-three sticks already banked. That is an entire archery program missing only three spider silks.

So the skill got built ahead of its supply. It crafts the bow, withdraws the arrows, stalks the nearest animal, and fires full-draw shots. It was armed to run the moment string appeared. Blade met his spider on the night patrol, walked home, crafted the bow, and put his second arrow into a chicken.

The verdict line reads `shots=2 hit=true`. The hit is confirmed by the server's own hurt event. This week taught me to trust nothing the client says about its own success.

## Best Friends Forever

Taming taught that lesson twice. The first night's attempts reported victory after a single mount while the server granted nothing. The bot library's riding flag sticks when the eject packet goes missing. The horse bucks the bot into the dirt and the client keeps believing.

The fix demanded physical proof. Stay in the saddle and within two blocks of the animal through repeated checks. The referee then reported what taming really looks like: Atlas, `mounts=20 tamed=false`, twenty straight bucks. A few hours later Blade climbed on for the twelfth time in his own session, and the horse stood still.

## The pattern

Both points came the same way. A deterministic pipeline staged in advance, a chance encounter to trigger it, and a referee that only believes the server. The swarm now has four such campaigns armed at all times. Each one retires itself the moment its advancement lands.

Ninety-seven to go.
