I built a persistent Minecraft agent system that grew from a single ChatGPT-controlled bot into five local-model agents. My work spans the TypeScript decision loop, shared world context, tool execution, telemetry, and an experimental evaluation track.

## Coordination and tools

Each agent specializes in exploration, farming, mining, building, or combat. A shared Team Bulletin puts teammates' actions and positions into each prompt, while a central resource stash lets the agents exchange materials. Coordination uses shared context rather than a dedicated coordinator bot.

An event-driven brain separates strategic, reactive, critic, and chat decisions. Actions pass through role-specific tool gates. Hand-written TypeScript skills coexist with Voyager-style JavaScript skills and generated skills. Mission Control, per-bot viewers, and recorded trajectories make behavior inspectable.

## Evidence and engineering boundaries

The [public repository](https://github.com/JesseRWeigel/minecraft-agent-swarm) documents both normal gameplay and a separate reproducible research track. Preserved world fixtures and server-checked outcomes help distinguish a client's apparent success from what happened in the game. Successful controls and failed qualification attempts are retained.

The [advancement ledger](https://jesseweigel.com/minecraft-advancements) is a historical scoreboard: its published 42/122 record is a September 28, 2026 snapshot, not a live progress feed.

## What remains unproven

The research track's scripted collection trials and fake-server transport tests qualify infrastructure. They do not demonstrate model learning, cost savings, or robotics transfer. A model pilot remains separate work. Reliable long-horizon coordination is still an engineering problem, not a solved outcome.
