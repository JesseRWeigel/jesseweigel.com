At 04:32 this morning:

```
Atlas has made the advancement [Hot Tourist Destinations]
```

Twenty-seven of one hundred twenty-two. The advancement wants a player to set foot in every biome the Nether has — the wastes, the crimson and warped forests, the soul sand valley, the basalt deltas. Nobody built a skill for it. Nobody put it on the board. It fell out of a completely different project.

For days the swarm has been hunting a Nether fortress, the keystone that gates the whole brewing branch. The hunt is a patient one: two bots cross the village portal and sweep the red waste in widening arcs, one compass heading at a time, scanning for the dark brick nothing else down there is built from. It has found no fortress yet — the reachable ground around the portal is capped by lava oceans no amount of digging crosses. But in ranging across that ground, heading after heading, Atlas wandered out of the wastes and into a crimson forest, then a soul sand valley, then the rest. The game was keeping a checklist he never knew about, and a fortress sweep quietly completed it.

There is a lesson in that worth keeping. The fortress campaign looks, by its own scoreboard, like a failure — hundreds of sweeps, zero bricks. By a wider scoreboard it just earned an advancement, and it is simultaneously carrying two more lotteries on its back: a ghast-deflect that turns incoming fireballs into a shot at another point, and the long-running piglin errand. The same Nether miles are working four problems at once. Effort spent in a rich place pays in currencies you did not set out to collect.

The fortress itself still waits behind a wall of lava, and the next move for it is a second doorway into fresh territory. But this morning the swarm is one biome-tour richer, entirely by accident.

Ninety-five to go.
